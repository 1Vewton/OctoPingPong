## Background

Table tennis, being a sport with relatively flexible venue requirements and adjustable
exercise intensity, is highly suitable for rehabilitation training and daily exercise among
special-needs groups. However, mainstream table tennis rackets on the market are all designed
around the hand size (palm width of approximately 8–10 cm, handle circumference of
approximately 8–12 cm) and strength standards of an average adult.

For patients with dwarfism (generally referring to a group whose adult height is below 147 cm
and whose hand size, arm length, and muscle strength are all markedly smaller than those of the
general population), existing rackets present the following pain points:

1. Weight overload: standard rackets typically weigh between 170 g and 200 g; for patients with
   weaker hand strength, the swing inertia is large, which readily causes strain to the wrist
   and elbow joints.
2. Handle discomfort: standard handles are too thick and too long, making it difficult for
   patients to switch flexibly between a "forehand grip" and a "backhand grip"; in some cases
   the handle cannot be held completely, posing a risk of the racket slipping out of the hand.
3. Imbalance of the centre of gravity: on ordinary rackets the centre of gravity lies close to
   the racket head; for patients with shorter arms the torque effect is amplified, markedly
   increasing the difficulty of control.

## Objectives

The Octo table tennis racket project aims to design a racket customised specifically for
patients with dwarfism, with lightweight construction as the core objective:

1. Weight-reduction target: control the total weight of the finished racket within 110–130 g
   (a 30 %–40 % reduction relative to standard rackets) to lower the joint load during play.
2. Grip optimisation: design an adjustable or ultra-slim short-handle structure suited to users
   with a palm width < 7 cm, ensuring a firm grip and flexible switching.
3. Rearward shift of the centre of gravity: through material and structural design, move the
   racket's balance point towards the handle, reducing the moment of inertia and improving the
   sensitivity of control.

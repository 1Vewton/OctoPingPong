# Experimental Safety Instructions

## 1 Introduction

This document is addressed to all experimental participants of the Octo table tennis racket
project. It aims to systematically identify the potential safety risks existing in each phase of
the project (material processing, chemical handling, and assembly/testing) and to specify the
corresponding protective measures and operating procedures. The project involves operations such
as woodworking, composite bonding, handle modification, and counterweight installation;
compliance with these instructions can effectively reduce the risk of personal injury and
property loss.

This project is carried out by a high-school student team, and the principle of "safety first"
runs throughout. All experimental operations must be conducted **with a guardian or supervising
teacher present**. Operations beyond one's personal skill range should be completed under
professional guidance or with external assistance.

## 2 General Safety Guidelines

### 2.1 Basic Requirements

- Before the experiment, the entire contents of these safety instructions must be read and
  understood, and confirmation given before operation begins.
- The experimental area should be kept tidy, well ventilated, and adequately lit.
- Eating, drinking, and horseplay are prohibited in the experimental area.
- Long hair must be tied back, loose clothing must be fastened, and jewellery or gloves that
  could be caught in machinery must not be worn.
- After the operation is completed, the worktop should be cleaned promptly, tools returned to
  their places, and waste sorted for disposal.
- Before using any electrical equipment, check that the power cord is intact, and operate only
  with dry hands.

## 3 Material Safety

### 3.1 Woodworking Safety

#### 3.1.1 Hazards of Wood Dust

**Risk**: the fine wood dust produced during the cutting and sanding of wood can be inhaled into
the lungs; long-term exposure may cause respiratory irritation, allergic reactions, and even more
serious lung diseases[^1]. The sensitising potential of dust differs among woods—the dust of
hardwoods such as Balsa, Ebony, and Rosewood is more highly sensitising[^2].

**Protective measures**:

- A **dust mask of N95 grade or above** must be worn during cutting/sanding.
- Give priority to operating outdoors or in a ventilated environment equipped with dust
  extraction.
- When sanding by hand, connecting a vacuum cleaner or using dust-collecting sandpaper is
  recommended.
- After the operation, wipe the worktop with a damp cloth to avoid raising dust by dry sweeping.
- The use of compressed air to blow away wood dust is strictly prohibited.

#### 3.1.2 Sharp Edges and Splinters of Wood

**Risk**: freshly cut or not finely sanded wood edges may have burrs, splinters, or sharp corners
that readily cause cuts or puncture wounds.

**Protective measures**:

- Wear cut-resistant gloves when handling and processing untreated timber.
- After cutting, promptly sand the burrs with sandpaper (#120–#240).
- Before handling bare-handed, lightly touch the edge with the back of the hand to confirm there
  are no sharp splinters.

### 3.2 Safety of Carbon Fibre and Composite Materials

#### 3.2.1 Carbon-Fibre Dust

**Risk**: the carbon-fibre filaments and dust produced when cutting or sanding carbon-fibre fabric
or fibreboard are highly conductive and **can damage electronic equipment**; at the same time,
carbon-fibre dust is mechanically irritating, may cause itching and rashes on skin contact, and
irritates the respiratory tract when inhaled[^3].

**Protective measures**:

- It is **strictly prohibited** to sand carbon fibre in an area where electronic equipment is not
  switched off or covered.
- Wear safety goggles, a dust mask, and long-sleeved work clothes during the operation.
- Use a wet-cutting method (spraying water on the fabric surface) to reduce the spread of dust.
- After the operation, use a lint roller to remove fibre debris from clothing.
- Discarded carbon-fibre fragments must be sealed and disposed of; they must not be mixed with
  ordinary household waste.

#### 3.2.2 Aramid (Kevlar) Fibre

**Risk**: aramid fibre tends to fray at the edges when cut, is difficult to cut, and the fibre
debris is likewise irritating.

**Protective measures**:

- Use sharp scissors or a rotary cutter to avoid fraying caused by pulling.
- Double-coated scissor blades are recommended to extend tool life.
- Wash hands after the operation to avoid skin itching caused by residual fibres.

### 3.3 Safety of Adhesives and Chemical Reagents

#### 3.3.1 Epoxy Resin

Epoxy resin consists of two components, a resin and a hardener, and undergoes an exothermic
curing reaction after mixing.

**Risks**:

- **Skin contact**: uncured epoxy resin can cause contact dermatitis and skin sensitisation; some
  individuals may develop an allergic reaction after repeated contact[^4].
- **Inhalation hazard**: the volatile amine compounds in the hardener irritate the respiratory
  tract.
- **Exothermic reaction**: when mixed in large quantities (more than 200 g at a time), the
  reaction releases heat violently, which can melt the container or even cause a fire.

**Protective measures**:

- Wear nitrile gloves when mixing epoxy resin; do not use latex gloves (some resins can permeate
  latex).
- Operate in a well-ventilated place, wearing an organic-vapour mask if necessary.
- Do not mix more than 50 g at a time; use metal or glass containers (avoid thin-walled plastic
  cups melting).
- If skin contact occurs, immediately wipe with **alcohol** and then wash with soapy water;
  **do not** wash a large area with organic solvents.
- Cured epoxy resin is non-toxic and harmless and can be disposed of as general waste.

#### 3.3.2 PVA Wood Glue

**Risk**: PVA glue is non-toxic but mildly irritating to mucous membranes; the wet floor before
drying and curing poses a tripping hazard.

**Protective measures**:

- If it splashes into the eyes, immediately rinse with clean water for 15 minutes.
- Lay disposable paper on the worktop to prevent glue droplets from forming a film.
- After use, wipe away excess glue with a damp cloth (it is difficult to remove once dry).

#### 3.3.3 Wood Wax Oil and Varnish/Lacquer

**Risk**: oil-based coatings contain volatile organic compounds (VOCs); inhalation in a confined
space can cause dizziness, nausea, and other discomfort.

**Protective measures**:

- Only apply outdoors or in a fume hood.
- Use water-based polyurethane instead of oil-based products to reduce the VOC risk.
- Wear nitrile gloves and a mask while brushing.
- After brushing, dry the object in a ventilated place for 24 hours before bringing it indoors
  for use.

### 3.4 Safety of Counterweight Materials

#### 3.4.1 Lead

**Risk**: lead and its compounds are toxic substances that can enter the body through inhalation
of lead dust or through hand-to-mouth ingestion. Lead poisoning causes irreversible damage to the
nervous system, the haematopoietic system, and the kidneys[^5]. **This project strictly prohibits
the use of bare lead material as a counterweight material.**

**Substitutes (in order of recommendation)**:

1. **Brass** — safe, easy to machine, density 8.4–8.7 g/cm³, close to 80 % that of lead.
2. **Stainless steel** — safe, density 7.8 g/cm³, easy to source.
3. **Tungsten alloy** — non-toxic, density as high as 18 g/cm³, but more expensive and difficult
   to machine.

**If lead must be used (for example, if the target counterweight can only be achieved with lead
beads or a lead column)**, the following additional procedures must be observed:

- The lead column must be completely encapsulated and sealed (e.g. heat-shrink-tube wrapping +
  epoxy sealing) to ensure there is no exposed surface.
- Machining lead material (e.g. drilling, cutting) must be carried out in a well-ventilated
  place, wearing a dust mask and nitrile gloves.
- After the operation, clean the hands and worktop with dedicated wipes; **it is prohibited** to
  touch food or the mouth.
- Lead-containing waste must be sealed, marked, and disposed of as hazardous waste.

#### 3.4.2 Machining of Brass and Stainless Steel

**Risk**: brass dust can cause respiratory irritation; metal burrs are sharp and easily cause
cuts.

**Protective measures**:

- Wear safety goggles and cut-resistant gloves when drilling/sanding metal parts.
- Brass dust is mildly toxic[^6]; wear a dust mask.
- After machining, clean metal debris from the worktop with a magnet or a lint roller.

## 4 Tool-Operation Safety

### 4.1 Hand Tools

#### 4.1.1 Coping Saw and Hacksaw

- The workpiece must be firmly fixed with a vise or a C-clamp; never hold the workpiece by hand
  while cutting.
- The saw blade must be tensioned appropriately, and the cutting direction must be away from the
  body.
- Maintain a steady pace while sawing to avoid the blade breaking and springing off due to sudden
  pushing.
- After use, release the blade tension and store it properly.

#### 4.1.2 File and Sandpaper

- A file must be used with a handle fitted; the pointed tang of a handle-less file can cause
  puncture injuries.
- Apply uniform pressure when filing, and do not apply force on the return stroke.
- When sanding, avoid excessive bending of the fingers to prevent friction-heat burns.
- Use a sandpaper block instead of gripping the sandpaper bare-handed—it is more efficient and
  safer.

#### 4.1.3 Utility Knife and Rotary Cutter

- Check whether the blade is sharp before each use—a blunt blade requires greater pushing force
  and is more prone to slipping out of control.
- **No part of the body** may be in the direction of the blade when cutting.
- Use a cutting mat to protect the worktop and prevent blade blunting.
- Use a dedicated tool or pliers when replacing blades; avoid bare-handed operation.
- Discarded blades must be wrapped or placed in a dedicated recycling container.

### 4.2 Power Tools

#### 4.2.1 Hand Drill and Drill Press

- Before drilling, ensure the workpiece is firmly clamped; **drilling while holding the workpiece
  by hand is strictly prohibited**.
- Long hair must be tucked into a cap, and sleeves rolled up or fastened.
- The drill bit must be sharp, and the drilling speed must match the material (high speed for
  wood, low speed for metal).
- Keep the drill bit vertical and apply uniform force while drilling; reduce the pushing force at
  the moment of breakthrough to prevent the workpiece from flying out.
- Replace the drill bit only after the power has been completely cut off.
- Wear safety goggles when operating a drill press; do not wear gloves near rotating parts.

#### 4.2.2 Electric Sander

- The sandpaper must be intact and undamaged; replace the sandpaper only after the power has been
  cut off.
- Apply moderate force when sanding; excessive pressure will overload the motor or tear the
  sandpaper.
- Clean the dust bag promptly to avoid dust accumulation.
- The sander's power cord must be kept away from the rotating working surface.

#### 4.2.3 CNC Router

- Carefully read the equipment user manual before operation.
- The workpiece must be reliably fixed to the worktable with double-sided tape or clamps; **the
  fixing step must not be omitted "just for a quick test"**.
- After every tool change, the tool must be re-zeroed (set zero point) to avoid tool collision.
- When setting the feed rate and spindle speed, refer to the material parameter table to avoid
  tool overload and breakage.
- **Do not open the protective cover** while the CNC is running, and do not touch the tool with
  hands or tools.
- The location of the emergency stop button (E-stop) must be confirmed before operation.
- After the run ends, wait until the spindle has completely stopped before cleaning the working
  area.
- CNC programming should first be verified in simulation mode, and executed only after the
  toolpath is confirmed to be correct.

#### 4.2.4 3D Printer (FDM)

- The printer must be placed on a stable, fire-resistant surface, away from flammable materials.
- When printing high-temperature filaments such as ABS, the room must be kept ventilated, or an
  enclosed printer must be used.
- The print-bed temperature can reach 60–110 °C; after printing, wait for the bed to cool to room
  temperature (<40 °C) before removing the model.
- Use a spatula to remove the model, with the direction away from the body, to avoid slipping and
  cutting the hand.
- Regularly clean residual filament from the nozzle to prevent dripping that affects print
  quality and generates smoke.
- Do not print overnight unattended (if long printing is required, ensure the smoke alarm is
  functioning properly).

## 5 Chemical-Experiment Safety

### 5.1 Adhesive Operating Procedures

- Mix and apply adhesives in a well-ventilated place.
- Use disposable plastic or paper cups as mixing containers, and mark the volume scale on the cup
  wall.
- After each batch of glue has cured, scrape off the residue with a putty knife; **do not pour it
  directly into the sink** (epoxy resin can clog pipes).
- If the container is found to be abnormally hot when mixing epoxy resin, immediately move it to a
  fire-resistant surface outdoors, away from combustibles.

### 5.2 Coating Operating Procedures

- Spraying varnish or paint must be carried out outdoors or in an area equipped with an exhaust
  system.
- Wear an organic-vapour respirator and chemical safety goggles when spraying.
- Use brushing instead of spraying to reduce aerosol generation.
- Discarded paint cans, brushes, and thinners must be sorted and disposed of as hazardous waste.

### 5.3 Emergency Handling

| Accident type | Handling method |
| :------- | :------- |
| **Chemical splash into the eyes** | Immediately rinse with an eyewash or clean water for at least 15 minutes, holding the eyelids open while rinsing; seek medical attention after rinsing |
| **Skin contact with epoxy/hardener** | Wipe with an alcohol pad, then wash thoroughly with soap and water; seek medical attention if redness, swelling, or itching occurs |
| **Dizziness from inhaling harmful gases** | Immediately move to a ventilated area, loosen the collar, and keep the airway clear; call emergency services if symptoms persist |
| **Small cuts/puncture wounds** | Rinse the wound with clean water → disinfect with iodophor → bandage with sterile dressing; seek medical attention promptly if the wound is deep or bleeding does not stop |
| **Burns (hot plate/hot nozzle)** | Rinse with running cold water for 15 minutes; do not apply toothpaste, soy sauce, or other folk remedies; do not puncture blisters, and cover with a sterile dressing |
| **Electrical equipment smoking/catching fire** | Immediately cut off the power → use a CO₂ or dry-powder fire extinguisher; **never** use water to extinguish the fire |

## 6 Electrical Safety

- All power tools should have their power cord and plug checked for integrity before use.
- Keep power sockets away from water sources and chemical-handling areas.
- Do not use equipment with damaged insulation or loose plugs.
- When unplugging, grip the plug body; do not pull on the power cord.
- When using multiple devices simultaneously, calculate the total power to ensure it does not
  exceed the socket's rated power (usually 2,200–2,500 W).
- Disconnect the power promptly after the tool has been used.

## 7 Laboratory Environmental Hygiene

### 7.1 Ventilation Requirements

- Woodworking: at least 6 air changes per hour (using an exhaust fan or opening windows for
  cross-ventilation).
- Adhesive/coating operations: use an exhaust fan to expel the airflow outward, and position
  personnel upwind.
- 3D printing: set up a dedicated ventilated printing area, or use an enclosed printer with HEPA
  filtration.

### 7.2 Waste Sorting and Disposal

| Waste type | Handling method |
| :--------- | :------- |
| Ordinary wood dust/wood blocks | Seal in a garbage bag and dispose of as general solid waste |
| Carbon-fibre debris | Seal the packaging, mark it "carbon-fibre waste", and dispose of it as industrial solid waste |
| Epoxy/glue cured blocks | After curing, they may be discarded as general solid waste; **do not** pour uncured glue into the sink |
| Discarded blades/drill bits | Wrap in cardboard and affix a warning label before discarding |
| Lead-containing counterweight leftovers | Seal and mark, and hand over to a professional institution for disposal as hazardous waste |
| Solvent/paint waste liquid | Collect in designated containers, label them, and hand them over periodically |
| 3D-printing support waste | PLA/PETG can be recycled as plastic; ABS is not recyclable and is disposed of as general solid waste |

### 7.3 Worktop Cleaning Procedures

- After each day's operation, clean wood dust, metal debris, and glue residue from the worktop.
- Use a vacuum cleaner to clean the floor (**do not** use a broom, to avoid raising dust).
- Wipe the worktop regularly with a damp cloth to remove fine dust.
- Clean and maintain tools before returning them to their places (clean the tooth gaps of files
  with a wire brush; wipe drill bits with an oiled cloth to prevent rust).

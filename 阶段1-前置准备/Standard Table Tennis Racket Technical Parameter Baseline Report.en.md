# Standard Table Tennis Racket Technical Parameter Baseline Report

## 1 Introduction

This report aims to systematically record the technical parameters of standard table tennis rackets as a comparison baseline for the design of the Octo table tennis racket. By compiling the equipment specifications of the International Table Tennis Federation (ITTF), the physical parameters of representative blades from mainstream brands (Butterfly, Stiga, etc.), and the weight data of standard rubbers, it provides a quantitative reference baseline for the subsequent lightweight design (target total weight 110–130 g).

The report covers the following content:

- The specification requirements for table tennis rackets in the ITTF and national standards
- The dimensions, weight, thickness, and structural parameters of mainstream blades
- The weight-data ranges of standard rubbers (topsheet + sponge)
- The typical total weight and weight distribution of a finished racket
- The industry baseline for the racket's centre of gravity (balance point)

---

## 2 ITTF and National-Standard Specifications

### 2.1 ITTF Competition Rules (2025 Edition)

According to Article 2.4 of the ITTF's *Laws of Table Tennis*, the relevant racket regulations are as follows[^1]:

| Item | Regulation |
| :-------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Size and shape | There is no restriction on the size, shape, or weight of the racket, but the blade shall be flat and rigid |
| Wood proportion | At least 85 % of the blade by thickness shall be of natural wood |
| Fibre-layer limit | The inner layers of the blade may be bonded with materials such as carbon fibre, glass fibre, or compressed paper, but each layer shall not exceed 7.5 % of the total blade thickness or 0.35 mm (whichever is the smaller) |
| Rubber thickness (pimples) | For ordinary pimpled rubber facing outwards, the total thickness including the adhesive shall be < 2.0 mm |
| Rubber thickness (sandwich) | For sandwich rubber, the total thickness including the adhesive shall be < 4.0 mm |
| Pimple density | ≥ 10 and ≤ 30 per square centimetre (pimpled rubber) |
| Rubber colour | When both sides are covered, the two sides shall not be the same colour; one side must be red and the other black |
| Surface gloss | The surface gloss of the blade and the rubber covering shall be < 24 degrees |

### 2.2 Chinese National Standards

GB/T 23115-2008 *Table Tennis Rackets* specifies the classification, requirements, and test methods for table tennis rackets for competition and practice[^2]. GB/T 9831-1988 (now withdrawn but of reference value) once classified rackets into superior-grade, first-grade, and acceptable products, and specified test methods for pimple geometric parameters and adhesion.

### 2.3 Industry Standard

T/STIC 110004-2021 *Professional Competition Table Tennis Rackets, Rubbers and Blades* further refines the technical requirements for professional competition equipment[^3].

## 6 Centre-of-Gravity (Balance-Point) Baseline

### 6.1 Method of Measuring the Centre-of-Gravity Position

The balance point of the centre of gravity is measured as follows: lay the blade flat on the edge of a vertically standing ruler, keeping the handle direction perpendicular to the ruler; once the balance point is found, measure the length from that point to the top end of the blade head and divide it by the total blade length to obtain the centre-of-gravity percentage[^13].

### 6.2 Industry Reference Range

| Racket type | CG position (distance from the racket-head top) | Percentage range |
| :------- | :--------------------: | :--------: |
| Single-sided penhold | 43 %–46 % of total length | Racket head relatively heavy |
| Two-sided penhold | 46 %–50 % of total length | CG slightly central |
| Shakehand (both sides) | 46 %–50 % of total length | CG slightly central |
| **Abnormal range (racket head too heavy)** | **< 43 %/46 %** | Difficult to control |
| **Abnormal range (racket head too light)** | **> 46 %/50 %** | Insufficient power |

> **Table 5: Centre-of-gravity range of standard rackets**

For a standard shakehand racket with a total length of about 257 mm (racket face 157 mm + handle 100 mm), a CG position of 46 %–50 % corresponds to about **118–128 mm** from the top end of the racket head; that is, the CG lies near the junction between the racket face and the handle, slightly towards the racket-head side.

## 7 Data Summary and Benchmarking against the Octo Design

### 7.1 Standard Baseline vs. Octo Target

| Parameter | Standard baseline (typical value) | Octo target | Change |
| :-------- | :--------: | :-----------: | :------------: |
| Total weight of finished racket | 170–200 g | **110–130 g** | **30 %–40 % weight reduction** |
| Blade weight | 85–95 g | **55–70 g** | 25 %–35 % reduction |
| Cut weight per side of rubber | 46–53 g | 25–35 g | Thin sponge/lightweight rubber required |
| Racket-face size | 157×150 mm | 141×135 mm | Reducing the racket-face size can save weight |
| Blade thickness | 5.5–7.0 mm | 5.0–5.5 mm | 10 %–20 % thinning |
| Handle circumference | 85–95 mm | **65–80 mm** | 15 %–25 % reduction |
| Handle length (shakehand) | 100 mm | **60–75 mm** | 25 %–40 % shortening |
| CG position | 46 %–50 % of total length | **Biased towards the handle** | Requires adjustment through materials and counterweights |

> **Table 6: Standard parameter baseline vs. Octo design target**

## 8 Conclusion

This report establishes the technical-parameter baseline of a standard table tennis racket. The main conclusions are as follows:

1. **Blade-weight baseline**: the weight of standard shakehand blades is concentrated in the **82–95 g** range, with the most representative Butterfly Viscaria at about 88 g. The Octo design target requires the blade weight to be reduced to 55–70 g, a reduction of about 25 %–35 %.
2. **Finished-racket total-weight baseline**: the total weight of a standard configuration ranges from **170–200 g**, of which the blade accounts for about 46 % and each of the two rubbers about 26 %. The Octo target total weight of 110–130 g means that all components must be substantially reduced in weight.
3. **Handle-dimension baseline**: a standard shakehand handle is about 100 mm long and about 85–95 mm in circumference. The Octo handle must be shortened to 60–75 mm and its circumference reduced to 65–80 mm, to suit users with a palm width < 7 cm.
4. **Centre-of-gravity baseline**: the CG of a standard shakehand racket lies at 46 %–50 % of the total length from the top end of the racket head. Octo must shift the CG towards the handle through material selection and structural design, to reduce the moment of inertia.
5. **ITTF compliance baseline**: the ITTF imposes no restriction on the size and shape of the racket, but has explicit requirements for the wood proportion (≥85 %), the fibre-layer thickness (≤7.5 % or 0.35 mm), and the rubber thickness (≤4.0 mm), etc.; the Octo design must be carried out within this rule framework.

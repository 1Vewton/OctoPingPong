# Research Report on Table Tennis Blade Materials

## 1 Introduction

The structure and material selection of a table tennis blade directly affect its core performance, including speed, spin, control, and feel. According to the regulations of the International Table Tennis Federation (ITTF), in a competition table tennis blade, natural wood shall account for not less than 85 % of the thickness; the remaining portion of not more than 15 % may consist of synthetic materials such as carbon fibre, aramid fibre, and glass fibre, with each adhesive layer not exceeding 7.5 % of the total blade thickness or 0.35 mm[^1].

This report systematically reviews the types, characteristics, and typical structural applications of the natural woods and synthetic fibre materials commonly used in table tennis blades, providing a technical reference for the material selection and structural design of the Octo table tennis racket. The report covers: **types of natural wood** (Hinoki, Kiri, Ayous, Limba, Koto, etc.), **synthetic fibre materials** (carbon fibre, aramid fibre, Zylon fibre, glass fibre, etc.), **common ply structures** (all-wood 5-ply/7-ply, 3+2 carbon blades, 5+2 arylate-carbon blades), and the influence of **fibre inner/outer positioning** on performance.

## 2 Types and Characteristics of Natural Wood

The natural woods used in table tennis blades can be classified by their structural position into **outer ply (veneer)**, **medial ply (intermediate ply / secondary)**, and **core ply (core)**. Different woods are suited to different layer positions because of differences in physical properties such as density, elastic modulus, hardness, and grain[^2].

### 2.1 Hinoki / Japanese Cypress

- **Scientific name**: *Chamaecyparis obtusa*
- **Origin**: Japan, Alishan in Taiwan, North America
- **Characteristics**: light, highly elastic, good toughness, straight and fine grain
- **Density**: about 0.40–0.50 g/cm³
- **Typical uses**: outer ply, single-ply Hinoki blade (1 ply), core
- **Suited play style**: loop-drive fast attack

Because of its excellent elasticity and soft hitting feel, Hinoki is widely used by Japanese blade manufacturers (such as Butterfly and Darker), commonly for the outer ply and core of high-end blades. The **single-ply Hinoki blade (1-ply Hinoki)** is a classic choice for the penhold single-sided loop style[^3].

### 2.2 Kiri / Paulownia

- **Scientific name**: *Paulownia tomentosa*
- **Origin**: China, Japan, Southeast Asia
- **Characteristics**: light and soft, moderate strength, fast-growing, good "through-blade" property
- **Density**: about 0.25–0.35 g/cm³
- **Typical uses**: core
- **Suited play style**: attacking blades (in combination with fibre)

Kiri is the most widely used core material in today's table tennis blades and is especially common in carbon/arylate-carbon blades. Its light and soft texture effectively reduces the total blade weight while providing a good dwell feel. With technological development, **Green Kiri**, because of its more tightly packed fibres and better support, has gradually replaced traditional paulownia as the mainstream core material[^4]. The combination of a Kiri core with external arylate-carbon (ALC) performs most strongly in the 40+ plastic-ball era; representative blades include the Butterfly Viscaria and the Fan Zhendong ALC.

### 2.3 Ayous / Obeche

- **Scientific name**: *Triplochiton scleroxylon*
- **Origin**: West Africa
- **Characteristics**: fine wood, excellent elasticity, slightly heavier than Kiri
- **Density**: about 0.35–0.45 g/cm³
- **Typical uses**: medial ply, core
- **Suited play style**: loop/all-round

Ayous is a typical core choice for European blades (such as Stiga OC and Clipper Wood); because of its good deformation capacity and "blade power", it is especially favoured by loop-style players. Compared with Kiri, an Ayous core deforms more strongly and produces a fuller ball arc, suiting play styles that emphasise spin[^5].

### 2.4 Limba

- **Scientific name**: *Terminalia superba*
- **Origin**: West Africa
- **Characteristics**: fine grain, good toughness, soft ball-contact feel
- **Density**: about 0.45–0.55 g/cm³
- **Typical uses**: outer ply
- **Suited play style**: loop/control

Limba is one of the most classic outer plies in European blades; representative models include the Stiga OC and the Butterfly Korbel. Its relatively soft material and long fibres provide excellent dwell feel and spin-generating ability, suiting close-to-mid-distance loop play.

### 2.5 Koto

- **Scientific name**: *Pterygota macrocarpa*
- **Origin**: Southeast Asia, Africa
- **Characteristics**: hard and brittle, long fibres, relatively high specific gravity
- **Density**: about 0.55–0.65 g/cm³
- **Typical uses**: outer ply
- **Suited play style**: fast attack / fast loop

Because of its relatively high hardness and crisp, rapid ball exit, Koto is often used as the outer ply of attacking blades. Typical representatives include the Butterfly Viscaria and the Boll Spirit. A Koto outer ply combined with arylate-carbon fibre provides sharp ball-exit speed and clear feel feedback[^6].

### 2.6 Other Common Woods

| Wood name | Origin | Characteristics | Typical use | Density (g/cm³) |
| :------- | :--- | :--- | :------- | :----------: |
| Spruce | Europe | Uniform grain, good elasticity | Medial ply | 0.40–0.50 |

## 3 Types and Characteristics of Synthetic Fibre Materials

The ITTF rules stipulate that the synthetic fibre materials in a blade shall not exceed 15 % by thickness. The fibre materials currently mainstream on the market include carbon fibre, aramid fibre, Zylon fibre, glass fibre, and their blended combinations[^7].

### 3.1 Carbon Fibre

- **Appearance**: black
- **Characteristics**: extremely high strength, low specific gravity, small deformation under impact, fast recovery
- **Typical effects**: increases blade hardness and ball-exit speed and enlarges the sweet spot, but the feel is on the hard side and the dwell time is short
- **Weave types**: woven carbon, unidirectional carbon, chopped carbon, carbon powder

Carbon fibre is the most classic synthetic material in table tennis blades. Woven carbon fibre is made by interlacing carbon filaments in both directions and provides isotropic strength; unidirectional carbon fibre arranges the carbon filaments in a single direction and provides strength only along the fibre direction, and it has gradually fallen out of use. Chopped carbon fibre and carbon powder are mainly used in low- and mid-range blades—they increase elasticity but with limited effect[^8].

### 3.2 Aramid / Arylate / Kevlar Fibre

- **Scientific name**: poly-para-phenylene terephthalamide
- **Appearance**: blue (blue arylate), red (red arylate), yellow (yellow arylate)
- **Characteristics**: extremely high toughness, high viscoelasticity, fast deformation recovery, good vibration absorption
- **Typical effects**: provides a soft hitting feel, prolongs dwell time, and enhances blade power and spin ability

Aramid fibre can be divided by colour into:

- **Blue arylate**: the most commonly used; strong toughness, soft feel, outstanding blade power, often blended with carbon into arylate-carbon (ALC).
- **Red arylate**: relatively high specific gravity, softest feel, weaker blade power; mostly found in all-round or defensive blades.
- **Yellow arylate**: more widely used in domestically produced blades; softer than blue arylate, lower cost, fast deformation recovery[^9].

Kevlar is the trademark name for aramid fibre registered by DuPont and is one type of aramid fibre.

### 3.3 Arylate-Carbon Blended Fibre (ALC / Aramid-Carbon)

- **Composition**: a woven fabric blending aramid fibre and carbon fibre in a certain ratio
- **Characteristics**: combines the flexibility of aramid and the rigidity of carbon, providing both good dwell capability and ball-exit speed
- **Typical effects**: pronounced blade power; suited to mid-to-close-distance fast attack combined with loop play

ALC is currently one of the most popular fibre combinations on the market; representative blades include the Butterfly Viscaria, the Timo Boll Spirit, and the Zhang Jike series[^10].

### 3.4 Zylon Fibre (ZLF / PBO)

- **Full name**: poly-p-phenylene-2,6-benzobisoxazole
- **Appearance**: gold
- **Characteristics**: more than twice the strength of aramid, extremely soft feel, lighter weight, excellent elasticity and recovery speed
- **Typical effects**: provides an extremely high speed/control ratio, with a soft feel and abundant power

ZLF can be used alone as a fibre layer or blended with carbon fibre into ZLC. Representative blades include the Butterfly Photino (ZLF) and the Boll ZLC[^11].

### 3.5 ZLC (Zylon + Carbon)

- **Composition**: a blend of Zylon fibre and carbon fibre, usually about 50 % Zylon + 50 % carbon
- **Characteristics**: 10 %–15 % lighter than ALC, softer feel, with the sweet spot and speed retained
- **Typical effects**: has the highest speed/control ratio; suited to attacking players who seek both feel and speed

### 3.6 Glass Fibre

- **Characteristics**: moderate strength, soft feel, closest to all-wood
- **Typical effects**: low cost, stabilises the feel, reduces individual variation
- **Uses**: support layer; used in all-round blades that need a soft feel

## 4 Ply Structures of the Blade

The ply structure of a table tennis blade ranges from 1 ply (single Hinoki) to 17 plies, but 5-ply and 7-ply structures are the most common. The more plies there are and the thinner each ply, the harder and faster the blade generally is[^12].

### 4.1 All-Wood Blades

#### 5-ply All-Wood

- **Typical structure**: outer ply—medial ply—core—medial ply—outer ply (symmetric structure)
- **Characteristics**: good control, clear feel, long dwell time, strong spin ability
- **Representative models**: Stiga OC (Koto + Spruce + Ayous), Butterfly Korbel (Limba + Limba + Ayous), Yasaka YE
- **Suited play style**: loop, all-round

#### 7-ply All-Wood

- **Typical structure**: outer ply—medial ply—secondary core—core—secondary core—medial ply—outer ply
- **Characteristics**: harder body, faster ball exit, stronger support
- **Representative models**: Stiga Clipper Wood (Limba + Ayous ×5 + Limba), Butterfly Chen Jing
- **Suited play style**: fast attack, fast loop

### 4.2 Fibre-Composite Blades

#### 3+2 Structure (3 wood + 2 fibre)

- **Typical structure**: outer ply—fibre—core—fibre—outer ply
- **Characteristics**: relatively thick body (6.5–7.4 mm), strong rigidity, rapid rebound
- **Representative models**: Butterfly Primorac Carbon (Hinoki + carbon + Kiri core), Sardius (thickened Primorac Carbon)
- **Suited play style**: speed-centred fast attack

#### 5+2 Structure (5 wood + 2 fibre) — Current Mainstream

- **Typical structure**: outer ply—fibre—medial ply—core—medial ply—fibre—outer ply
- **Characteristics**: two more wood layers than the 3+2 structure, giving a softer feel and a longer dwell time
- **Representative models**: Butterfly Viscaria (Koto + arylate-carbon + Ayous + Kiri core), Boll Spirit, Zhang Jike ALC
- **Suited play style**: fast attack combined with loop; loop-drive fast attack

### 4.3 Comparison of Structure Types

| Structure type | Plies | Typical thickness (mm) | Typical weight (g) | Speed | Control | Sweet spot |
| :------- | :-: | :-----------: | :----------: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Single Hinoki | 1 | 8–10 | 80–95 | ★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★ |
| 5-ply all-wood | 5 | 5.5–6.5 | 80–90 | ★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★ |
| 7-ply all-wood | 7 | 6.0–7.0 | 85–95 | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★ |
| 3+2 carbon blade | 5 | 6.5–7.4 | 85–100 | ★★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★★★★ |
| 5+2 arylate-carbon blade | 7 | 5.5–6.2 | 85–95 | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★★★ |

## 5 Influence of Inner vs. Outer Fibre Positioning on Performance

The position of the fibre layer within the blade has a significant influence on performance, and can be divided into **outer** and **inner** types[^13].

### 5.1 Outer Fibre (Fiber under Veneer)

- **Definition**: the fibre layer lies below the outer ply and above the medial ply
- **Characteristics**: direct ball exit, fast speed, feel on the hard side, large sweet spot
- **Suitable for**: attacking players who emphasise speed and rebound-assisted power
- **Representatives**: Butterfly Viscaria, Boll ALC (outer)

### 5.2 Inner Fibre (Fiber near Core)

- **Definition**: the fibre layer lies below the medial ply and above the core (or on both sides of it)
- **Characteristics**: retains more of the wood feel, longer dwell time, and a sense of "extra gear" acceleration after applying force
- **Suitable for**: loop-style players who emphasise spin and arc
- **Representatives**: Butterfly Innerforce ZLC (inner)

### 5.3 Position Comparison

| Parameter | Outer fibre | Inner fibre |
| :--- | :------: | :------: |
| Ball-exit speed (low force) | Fast | Moderate |
| Ball-exit speed (high force) | Fast | Very fast (extra acceleration) |
| Feel clarity | Hard, direct | Soft, natural |
| Dwell time | Short | Long |
| Spin ability | Moderate | Strong |
| Sweet-spot size | Large | Moderate-to-large |

## 6 Case Studies of the Material Structure of Classic Blades

The following lists several representative classic blades and analyses their material combinations and structural design logic.

### 6.1 Butterfly Viscaria

| Layer position | Material |
| :--- | :--- |
| Outer ply (plies 1, 7) | Koto |
| Fibre (plies 2, 6) | ALC (arylate-carbon weave) |
| Medial ply (plies 3, 5) | Ayous |
| Core (ply 4) | Kiri |
| Total thickness | 5.8 mm |
| Weight | approx. 87–92 g |

**Design features**: the golden combination of outer arylate-carbon + Kiri core; balanced attack and defence, combining fast attack and loop; one of the most successful blades of the 40+ plastic-ball era.

### 6.2 Stiga OC (Offensive Classic)

| Layer position | Material |
| :--- | :--- |
| Outer ply (plies 1, 5) | Dyed Koto |
| Medial ply (plies 2, 4) | Spruce |
| Core (ply 3) | Ayous |
| Total thickness | approx. 5.8 mm |
| Weight | approx. 82–88 g |

**Design features**: a classic 5-ply all-wood loop blade; the Spruce medial plies provide an excellent spring effect and the Ayous core provides abundant blade power; it is a loop weapon for the three-quarter-distance game.

### 6.3 Butterfly Primorac Carbon

| Layer position | Material |
| :--- | :--- |
| Outer ply (plies 1, 5) | Hinoki |
| Fibre (plies 2, 4) | Woven carbon |
| Core (ply 3) | Kiri |
| Total thickness | 7.1 mm |
| Weight | approx. 92–100 g |

**Design features**: a classic representative of the 3+2 structure; the carbon fibre lies below the outer ply, giving strong rigidity and fast rebound. The Hinoki outer ply provides a soft touch that neutralises the overly hard feel of the carbon.

## 7 Implications for the Material Selection of the Octo Table Tennis Racket

Combined with the research data of [Hand-Size and Grip-Strength Study of Dwarfism Patients](./Research%20Report%20on%20Hand-Size%20and%20Grip-Strength%20of%20Dwarfism%20Patients.en.md), the material selection of the Octo table tennis racket should focus on the following directions:

### 7.1 Weight-Reduction Strategy

| Measure | Expected weight reduction | Points to note |
| :--- | :------: | :------- |
| Use a lightweight core (Kiri) | 10–15 g | Must be combined with fibre layers to ensure stiffness |
| Adopt a thin-blade design (5.0–5.5 mm) | 5–10 g | Too thin may affect the dwell feel and strength |
| Use lightweight fibre (ZLF/ZLC instead of traditional carbon fibre) | 5–10 g | Higher cost, but better feel |
| Shorten the handle to 60–75 mm | 3–5 g | Meets the grip requirement for a palm width < 7 cm |

### 7.2 Centre-of-Gravity Rearward-Shift Strategy

- A **gradient-density design** may be used in the core (low-density light wood/Ayous on the racket-head side, with an integrated counterweight on the handle side).
- A **cavity or adjustable counterweight chamber** is designed inside the handle, allowing users to adjust the balance point according to their individual arm length and strength.

### 7.3 Balance between Feel and Support

- A **5+2 outer structure** or an **inner-fibre structure** is recommended, ensuring sufficient support while reducing the overall weight through the optimised choice of the fibre layers.
- Consider using **ZLF or ALC fibre** instead of traditional pure carbon fibre, to obtain a softer feel and better spin ability, and to reduce the impact of vibration transmission on the upper-limb joints.

## 8 Conclusion

This report systematically reviews the types, performance characteristics, and typical structural applications of the natural woods (Hinoki, Kiri, Ayous, Limba, Koto, Spruce, etc.) and synthetic fibre materials (carbon fibre, aramid fibre, ALC, ZLC, ZLF, glass fibre, etc.) used in table tennis blades. The main conclusions are as follows:

1. **Natural woods have a clear division of roles by layer position**: the outer ply determines the ball-contact feel (Hinoki soft, Koto hard and crisp, Limba good dwell); the medial ply provides support and elasticity (Spruce spring effect, Ayous strong toughness); the core determines the blade power and weight (Kiri light and transparent, Ayous strong deformation).
2. **Fibre materials provide performance extension**: carbon fibre increases speed and sweet-spot size; aramid fibre increases dwell time and spin; blended fibres such as ALC/ZLC combine the advantages of both and are the current mainstream choice.
3. **Ply structure and fibre position determine the performance orientation**: the 5+2 outer arylate-carbon structure is the dominant design of the current 40+ plastic-ball era, balancing speed, spin, and control.
4. **For the lightweight requirements of the Octo project**, priority should be given to a combination of a Kiri core + lightweight fibre (ZLF/ALC), and the total weight should be further reduced to the target range of 110–130 g through design measures such as shortening the handle, optimising the blade thickness, and gradient density.
